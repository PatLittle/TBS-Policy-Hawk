# Access to Information Implementation Notice 2026-01: Managing Records in Digital Collaboration Environments

## 1. Effective date

This implementation notice takes effect on November 2, 2026.

## 2. Authorities

This implementation notice is issued pursuant to paragraphs 70(1)(a) and (c) of the [Access to Information Act](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/a-1/FullText.html).

## 3. Purpose

This implementation notice provides guidance to government institutions, employees, and Access to Information and Privacy (ATIP) officials on managing transitory records created in digital collaboration environments, with particular emphasis on Microsoft Teams and tools in the Microsoft 365 (M365) suite. The principles and approaches described in this notice may also be applied to other digital collaboration tools and environments, as appropriate. This implementation notice addresses the relationship between information management (IM) and the right of access under the Act and offers practical guidance to help institutions better manage transitory records captured by access to information (ATI) requests and other record preservation processes, such as legal holds.

## 4. Context

Adopting digital collaboration tools across the Government of Canada (GC) has fundamentally changed the way employees communicate and create records. Platforms such as Microsoft Teams and other M365 services have become primary channels for daily communication, coordination and information-sharing, including interactions that would traditionally have taken place in person or over the phone without generating records simultaneously. Working with these tools produces an increasingly large volume of digital records that employees may not realize are subject to the same recordkeeping obligations as more traditional forms of government records.

Under the Act, a record is defined as “any documentary material, regardless of medium or form.” Whether content created in a digital collaboration environment constitutes a record, and whether it is a record of business value or a transitory record, depends on the substance and context of the content rather than the format in which it was created. The fact that a message is created in Microsoft Teams, another M365 application or email does not, in itself, determine whether it is a record of business value or a transitory record.

While these tools improve digital collaboration and productivity, they also present significant IM challenges. They produce large volumes of records and other content, much of which consists of brief exchanges, status updates, quick coordination messages, and informal transactions that have no lasting operational value and may be transitory. This can lead to a growing number of transitory records that, if left unmanaged, can substantially increase the time and resources required to preserve and retrieve them.

### 4.1 Records of business value and transitory records

Sections 4.7 and 4.8 of the [Directive on Service and Digital](https://www.tbs-sct.canada.ca/pol/doc-eng.aspx?id=32601) establish, respectively, that employees are responsible for documenting their activities and decisions of business value and that managers are responsible for informing employees of their duty to document their activities and decisions of business value. Some records created in a digital collaboration environment, such as a decision communicated in a Microsoft Teams message or a meaningful commitment made in a meeting chat, may have business value. As set out in [Appendix E: Identifying and Recognizing Information and Data of Business Value](https://www.canada.ca/en/government/system/digital-government/guideline-service-digital.html#ToC10) of the Guideline on Service and Digital:

Information and data of business value is defined in the Government of Canada as “published and unpublished materials, regardless of medium or form, that are created or acquired because they enable and document decision-making in support of programs, services and ongoing operations, and support departmental reporting, performance and accountability requirements.” Any information and data that is not identified as having business value is considered transitory.

Library and Archives Canada’s (LAC’s) [Disposition Authorization for Transitory Records (2016/001)](https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/services/government/information-disposition/records/multi-institution-disposition-authorizations/2016-001-da-transitory-records.html) indicates that:

Transitory records are not of business value. They may include records that serve solely as convenience copies of records held in a government institution repository, but do not include any records that are required to control, support, or document the delivery of programs, to carry out operations, to make decisions, or to provide evidence to account for the activities of government at any time.

The Treasury Board of Canada Secretariat (TBS) [Guidance for Employees of the Government of Canada: Information Management Basics](https://www.tbs-sct.canada.ca/pol/doc-eng.aspx?id=16557&section=HTML) lists the following examples of transitory information:

- working drafts of no particular significance that were never formally circulated
- annotated drafts where annotations become part of a subsequent version and do not provide evidence of decisions related to the evolution of the final document
- a copy of a document kept for ease of reference or convenience only
- information that lacks logical or coherent organization and therefore does not have context
- data that has been used for an update process (including batch processing) and that is no longer needed to serve as backup or to support reconstruction of the master file or database
- casual communications such as invitations to lunch
- personal documents stored on your computer or in your work area

Appropriately disposing of transitory records is a core element of sound IM. Retaining records beyond their useful life contributes to accumulating material that has no value but that can nonetheless be captured by preservation processes. This over-retention can impose unnecessary costs on institutions, delay the timely processing of ATI requests, and may increase risks to security or privacy. In all cases, proper IM is underpinned by the responsibility to ensure that records that have business value are preserved and that information relevant to ATI, privacy or litigation processes is not deleted.

In Microsoft Teams and M365 environments, institutions may retain large volumes of chat-based communications longer than necessary where retention and disposition controls have not been appropriately configured or employees have not been trained in distinguishing transitory records from records of business value. Although legal holds appropriately suspend normal disposition processes for records relevant to actual or anticipated litigation, prolonged or broadly scoped holds may increase the volume of records retained for longer than operationally necessary. Cloud-based services may preserve electronically stored information in ways that make it readily accessible through search and Microsoft’s eDiscovery tools, resulting in unnecessary record retention that is not relevant to the legal hold, falls outside its defined scope, and would otherwise be eligible for disposition.

### 4.2 Information management responsibilities: records retention and disposition

Government institutions subject to the [Library and Archives of Canada Act](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/l-7.7/FullText.html) must only destroy records as authorized in writing by the Librarian and Archivist of Canada. LAC offers recommendations on retention but does not define or approve specific retention practices for other departments.

LAC’s [Guidelines on Documented Disposition of Records](https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/services/government/information-disposition/management/guidelines/documented-disposition-records.html) provide additional advice to departmental officials on establishing and implementing disposition processes for all records in respect of section 4.4.8 of the Directive on Service and Digital, which requires developing a documented disposition process and on accounting for compliance with sections 12 and 13 of the Library and Archives of Canada Act, which pertain to records disposal (for example, destruction or transfer). Although the advice is primarily for GC organizations to which the [Policy on Service and Digital](https://www.tbs-sct.canada.ca/pol/doc-eng.aspx?id=32603) applies (that is, departments as defined in section 2 of the [Financial Administration Act](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/F-11/)), other organizations are also encouraged to follow the recommendations as appropriate.

The guidelines outline that for GC institutions, disposition is typically fulfilled by destruction, transfer to LAC, or alienation from the control of the GC to an outside party, and that transitory records can be destroyed at the end of their usefulness. LAC’s Disposition authorization for transitory records (2016/001) may be applied at any time to transitory records that meet the criteria for destruction under the terms of that authorization, except where an Institution-Specific Disposition Authorization contains instructions to the contrary. LAC’s [Generic Valuation Tools](https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/services/government/information-disposition/management/generic-valuation-tools/overview.html) (GVTs) also provide federal institutions with standardized advice and guidance on managing records related to a range of common business activities.

In addition, specific statutory and regulatory requirements may establish minimum retention periods for certain records. For example, section 4 of the [Privacy Regulations](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/sor-83-508/FullText.html) requires government institutions to retain personal information that has been used for an administrative purpose for at least two years following the last time it was used for that purpose, unless the individual consents to its disposal. Where a request for access to the personal information has been received, the information must be retained until the individual has had the opportunity to exercise all their rights under the Privacy Act. Section 7 of the Privacy Regulations further requires heads of government institutions to retain, for at least two years following the receipt of a request for access to personal information under paragraph 8(2)(e) of the Privacy Act, a copy of the request and a record of any information disclosed pursuant to the request.

TBS supports departments by issuing guidance and best practices but does not direct or approve department-specific retention decisions. Section 4.4 of the Directive on Service and Digital establishes strategic IM and recordkeeping responsibilities for departmental chief information officers, in collaboration with other departmental officials as necessary. These responsibilities include establishing, implementing and maintaining retention periods for all information and data, as appropriate, according to format, and developing a documented disposition process and performing regular disposition activities for all information and data, as required.

Ultimately, departments are responsible for determining and documenting retention periods for records under their control based on approved appraisal processes that consider operational, legal, regulatory, accountability and risk requirements. Departments are also responsible for ensuring that disposition actions are authorized, documented and carried out in accordance with established records management policies and procedures.

### 4.3 Legal holds and record preservation

A legal hold, also known as a litigation hold, is a temporary measure initiated by a notice or communication from the Department of Justice Canada to GC institutions that suspends the normal disposition of records deemed to be relevant to ongoing or reasonably anticipated litigation involving the government. Legal holds require GC departments to identify, preserve and collect records, as necessary, for the Department of Justice Canada to review and then produce as part of court proceedings. It applies to both paper and digital records or documents.

There are parallels between legal holds and ATI requests in that both may require potentially responsive records to be preserved. Accumulating transitory records, including chat messages in collaboration tools, can substantially increase the volume of records that must be searched and reviewed, and can cause significant effort to be spent on records that are ultimately determined not to be relevant to the legal matter or to be outside the scope of the ATI request.

It is important for government institution employees to note that eDiscovery in the Microsoft Purview portal can reach across supported M365 services to identify and preserve records. Records that are subject to a Microsoft Purview hold will be preserved even if they are deleted from a user’s visible interface.

### 4.4 Records under the control of a government institution

Under section 4 of the Act and the [Access to Information Act Extension Order, No. 1](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-89-207/page-1.html), Canadian citizens or permanent residents within the meaning of subsection 2(1) of the [Immigration and Refugee Protection Act](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-2.5) and all individuals and all corporations present in Canada have a right to and shall, on request, be given access to any record “under the control of” a government institution. While this expression is not defined in the Act, it has been broadly interpreted by the courts. In determining whether a record is under the control of a government institution, some of the factors to be considered include whether:

- it is held by the institution, whether at headquarters or at a regional, satellite or other office, either within or outside Canada, or at an offsite location such as a private storage facility or a federal records centre (records storage facility administered by LAC containing records that remain under the control of institutions)
- it is held elsewhere on behalf of the institution (for example, records maintained by agents, consultants or other contracted service providers), or
- the institution is authorized to grant or deny access to the record, to govern its use, and to dispose of it in accordance with applicable disposition authorizations issued by LAC

Control also depends on whether the contents of the records relate to a departmental matter and are relevant to the mandate, obligations, operations and functions of the institution, as opposed to records that are purely personal in nature. Records that are purely personal in nature are not under the control of the institution for the purposes of ATI or personal information requests, even if they are sent or received on work devices or servers. For example, this could include communications relating to personal medical appointments, messages exchanged with a child’s teacher or caregiver, or correspondence about personal activities outside of work hours. In more serious circumstances, a public servant may use their work email account to seek help because they feel unsafe at home. Their messages may not be under the institution’s control. Refer to [Access to Information and Privacy Implementation Notice 2024-01: Upholding Privacy and Safety of Public Servants](https://www.canada.ca/en/treasury-board-secretariat/services/access-information-privacy/access-information-privacy-notices/2024-01-upholding-privacy-safety-public-servants.html) for additional information on managing and processing delicate ATI and personal information requests where the information sought causes a public servant to fear for their safety because of who they are, and not the type of work they do.

ATI and personal information requests are not intended to capture records about non-institutional matters that happen to be in the physical possession of federal institutions. Responsibility for determinations on control rests solely with the institution’s delegated officials; it does not rest with the institution’s Offices of Primary Interest (OPIs) or with anyone else. Institutions are encouraged to have procedures in place to examine potentially responsive records and make determinations on control without inadvertently taking control of records they have no authority to possess. In straightforward cases, a visual examination of records by the ATIP office may be sufficient to confirm whether they are under the institution’s control, without retaining copies. Further guidance on making determinations with respect to control can be found in the [Access to Information Manual](https://www.canada.ca/en/treasury-board-secretariat/services/access-information-privacy/access-information/access-information-manual.html), the [Personal Information Request (PIR) Manual](https://www.canada.ca/en/treasury-board-secretariat/services/access-information-privacy/privacy/privacy-policies-guidance/pir-manual.html) and the Information Commissioner of Canada’s [Interpretation: control of records](https://www.oic-ci.gc.ca/en/interpretation-control-records). ATIP offices may also wish to consult their legal services when determining whether records on a particular subject matter could be under the control of the institution.

Records held in M365 environments, including Microsoft Teams chat histories and channel messages, may fall within the scope of ATI requests depending on the wording of the request and on whether employees use these platforms. It is a best practice for institutions to take an intentional approach to determining whether such records are under their control and fall within the scope of a given request, while also ensuring that searches are reasonable based on the nature of the request.

### 4.5 Obstructing the right of access

Subsection 67.1(1) of the Act makes it an offence to take any of the following actions with the intent to deny the right of access to information in records under the control of a government institution:

- destroy, mutilate or alter a record
- falsify a record or make a false record
- conceal a record, or
- direct, propose, counsel or cause any person in any manner to do anything mentioned in any of paragraphs (a) to (c)

Every person who contravenes subsection 67.1(1) is guilty of:

- an indictable offence and liable to imprisonment for a term not exceeding two years or to a fine not exceeding $10,000, or to both, or
- an offence punishable on summary conviction and liable to imprisonment for a term not exceeding six months or to a fine not exceeding $5,000, or to both

Government institutions subject to the Act must also comply with supporting requirements in TBS policy instruments. For instance, section 4.3.15 of the [Policy on Access to Information](https://www.tbs-sct.canada.ca/pol/doc-eng.aspx?id=12453) requires heads of government institutions or their delegates to ensure that appropriate procedures are in place to address any alleged obstruction of the right of access under the Act, including conduct described in subsections 67(1) and 67.1(1) of the Act.

Similarly, section 4.1.45 of the [Directive on Access to Information Requests](https://www.tbs-sct.canada.ca/pol/doc-eng.aspx?id=18310&section=html) requires heads of government institutions or their delegates to establish internal procedures to address alleged or suspected obstructions of the right of access. It also establishes requirements for all government institution employees to:

- report any suspected obstruction of the right of access (section 4.2.9 of the directive)
- be trained on their obligations under the Act, including as they relate to the offences described in subsections 67(1) and 67.1(1) (section B.2.1.8 of the directive)

Government institutions must ensure that internal procedures outline measures for investigating allegations of obstruction under the Act (section 4.1.45.2 of the directive) and are also encouraged to ensure that their procedures support timely investigation of any suspected unauthorized or undocumented disposition activity, provide mechanisms to immediately suspend any record destruction or alteration, and establish clear reporting and escalation processes for employees who believe they have been instructed to contravene records management, legal hold, or access to information obligations.

In all circumstances, section 67.1 of the Act prohibits the destruction of records in anticipation of a request for access under the Act or during the processing of such a request. Section 67.1 can be contravened even when no formal access request has been received by the institution if there is a clear intent to obstruct the right of access. Regardless of whether records are transitory or qualify for destruction under a disposition authorization, employees must not destroy any records if they are aware that a request for access to the records has been received or is expected.

### 4.6 Failure to comply with civil litigation disclosure obligations

Disclosing documentary evidence is a central feature of civil litigation procedures in all Canadian jurisdictions. Destroying or altering relevant documents, known as spoliation of evidence, undermines a fair trial process and interferes with the quest for the truth in judicial proceedings. A finding of spoliation entails that relevant documents were intentionally destroyed with the objective of adversely impacting the outcome of a case. Where potentially relevant documents are destroyed, altered or lost, the GC may face allegations of spoliation of evidence and court sanctions which may seriously compromise the Attorney General of Canada’s ability to properly represent the government. When a party destroys or hides relevant evidence, courts must presume that it was unfavourable to that party. The Courts also has discretion to impose a wide variety of other sanctions that range from dismissal of action, striking out a pleading, awarding costs and punitive damages, exclusion of evidence, and even contempt of court if the act of spoliation contravenes a court order.

## 5. Guidance

### 5.1 Using digital collaboration tools

Microsoft Teams and similar collaboration tools are designed to facilitate quick, informal communication and coordination. They are generally not designated corporate repositories for records of business value. Employees are encouraged not to use Microsoft Teams channels, chats or other direct messages to create, share or finalize records that document decisions, approvals, advice, commitments or other government actions of business value.

Nonetheless, when an informal exchange in a collaboration tool gives rise to a record of business value, such as when a decision is communicated in a Microsoft Teams message or when a meaningful commitment is made in a chat, employees are responsible for ensuring the record is saved in a designated corporate repository, such as a SharePoint library, GCdocs or another designated records management system. Once the record of business value has been captured and saved in a designated corporate repository and the original message or exchange in the collaboration tool no longer has independent business value, it can be treated as transitory. If the original message is responsive to a legal hold or ATI request, it must be preserved even if it is transitory. It is also a best practice for employees to organize, categorize and store records of business value in a designated corporate repository as soon as possible and to delete transitory records in accordance with IM policy and disposition authorizations on a regular basis if they are not related to an active or anticipated legal hold or responsive to an ATI request.

### 5.2 Proactively preserving records of business value and disposing of transitory records

Responsibility for appropriately documenting exchanges of business value rests with employees, who are encouraged to apply the same judgment to collaboration tool messages as they would to any other form of communication. Employees are also encouraged to treat the proactive and timely disposal of transitory records as an important part of sound IM, alongside the responsibility to preserve records of business value. These objectives are complementary, and good IM requires actively managing both ends of the information life cycle.

Transitory records, including Microsoft Teams chat messages and channel posts that do not document decisions or actions of business value, may be disposed of as soon as they are no longer required. Once an ATI request is received or a legal hold is put in place, any relevant transitory records cannot be disposed of. In practice, this means encouraging employees to review their Microsoft Teams message history, chat logs and related records periodically to dispose of those that are clearly transitory.

To support employees in managing their records, institutions are encouraged to provide clear and practical guidance on what constitutes a record of business value in the context of their specific programs and activities, on specific methods that can be used to save or transfer records to a designated corporate repository, and to incorporate this guidance into employee onboarding and training. Institutions are also encouraged to issue clear guidance to employees on retention periods for common types of collaboration tool records, aligned with applicable institutional retention schedules.

Proactively disposing of records that are legitimately transitory and that do not relate to an ongoing or anticipated ATI request or legal hold directly contributes to the efficiency and integrity of the institution’s ATI response and legal preservation processes. Institutions that systematically manage their transitory records will spend significantly less time and effort processing ATI requests and legal holds because the volume of responsive records will more accurately reflect the institution’s actual business holdings.

Records that have been lawfully disposed of before an ATI request is anticipated or received are not subject to the right of access. The obligation to search for and produce records under the Act applies only to records that exist at the time a request is received. Accordingly, timely and appropriate disposal of transitory records before a request is received is consistent with sound IM practices.

### 5.3 Configuring retention and deletion policies in M365

One of the most effective ways for institutions to manage transitory records in the M365 environment is to configure automated retention and deletion policies through Microsoft Purview or similar tools. These policies can be set to automatically delete Microsoft Teams chat messages and channel posts after a defined period, subject to applicable retention requirements and to the absence of a hold, helping to prevent transitory records from accumulating indefinitely.

Institutions are encouraged to establish governance processes with their IM authorities, OPIs, senior governance bodies, and information technology services to develop, implement, and periodically review retention and disposition controls that reflect their approved retention requirements. As a best practice, shorter default retention periods for informal collaboration tool messages can reduce the volume of transitory records unnecessarily captured by ATI requests and legal holds. Legal services and ATIP officials may be well positioned to provide advice on legal, regulatory, discovery and access requirements, while retention and disposition decisions remain subject to the institution’s approved information governance and records management framework.

### 5.4 Scope of access to information searches in collaboration environments

Subsection 4(2.1) of the Act requires government institutions to respond to ATI requests in an accurate, complete and timely manner, which includes searching for and locating all responsive records. Section 6 of the Act requires requests to provide sufficient detail to enable an experienced employee of the institution to identify the record with a reasonable effort. Section 4.2.2 of the directive requires employees of government institutions to make every reasonable effort to locate and retrieve requested records under the control of the government institution. Section 4.2.3 of the directive requires them to ensure that searches for records are comprehensive and consider both the letter and spirit of the request.

While ATIP offices provide guidance and oversight, OPIs are responsible and accountable for identifying and retrieving records that are responsive to an ATI request. Searches should be reasonable and informed by the subject matter, time frame and wording of the request. When there is a reasonable basis to believe that responsive records may reside in Microsoft Teams, SharePoint, Exchange Online or other M365 repositories, OPIs are encouraged to consult with IM, information technology and ATIP officials to determine the most appropriate search approach. Searches of backup, preservation, compliance or eDiscovery repositories may be considered when there is a reasonable basis to believe that responsive records exist in those locations and where such searches are reasonable based on the subject matter, time frame and apparent spirit of a given request.

Understanding the technical capabilities of M365 is important for assessing what is and is not reasonable to search. Microsoft Teams messages are not stored exclusively where they appear to be; behind the visible chat interface, all Microsoft Teams and group chat messages are journaled into folders within the Exchange Online mailboxes of each participant. These mailbox folders are not accessible to end users or administrators through standard channels but are designed for compliance and eDiscovery access through Microsoft Purview. M365 eDiscovery tools can search and filter by specific users, date ranges, message types, keywords and other parameters. These searches can locate and export records in a structured format but do not otherwise restore deleted records.

The concept of backup tapes or backup storage locations in existing ATIP jurisprudence and guidance may not map neatly onto the technical architecture of cloud-based platforms such as M365 that do not use backup tapes in a traditional sense. Searching the institution’s backup storage locations is not required as a matter of course but could be necessary to locate responsive records depending on the wording and scope of the ATI request. Such a search may also be contemplated when officials have reason to believe that responsive records may exist exclusively in compliance storage and have been deleted from active view, or when officials have concerns about the completeness or reliability of an OPI’s search.

Under subsection 4(3) of the Act and section 3 of the Access to Information Regulations, an institution may be required to create records from electronic systems when they can be produced using computer hardware, software and technical expertise normally used by the institution, unless the record’s production unreasonably interferes with the institution’s operations. In most cases, searching for and retrieving existing records from M365 repositories would not constitute creating a new record; however, requests that require information to be compiled, correlated, extracted or reported in a form that does not already exist, such as an audit log or aggregated metadata report, may engage the obligation to create a record. When determining whether the obligation applies, institutions must consider the technical effort required, whether the necessary tools and expertise are available in the normal course of operations, and whether producing the record would unreasonably interfere with those operations.

Searches in digital collaboration environments may identify records that are purely personal in nature and are not under the institution’s control. To avoid improperly retrieving records that are not under their control and in cases where it may be relevant, it is a best practice for institutions to specify in their tasking messages that responsive records of a purely personal nature must not be retrieved or submitted to the ATIP office. Records that discuss departmental matters but also include personal messages must be retrieved for processing, as such messages could be under the control of the government institution even though they contain personal information that is not related to government business. When there is doubt as to whether certain records are under an institution’s control, pursuant to section 4.1.20 of the directive and to section 4.1.1 of the [Directive on Personal Information Requests and Correction of Personal Information](https://www.tbs-sct.canada.ca/pol/doc-eng.aspx?id=32590), the institution’s delegated officials under the Act and Privacy Act must review the records and make a determination on control.

In the event of a complaint under the Act about the adequacy of the search, the institution may be expected to demonstrate that reasonable steps were taken to identify and retrieve responsive records. This includes documenting decisions on which repositories were searched and on which search terms and parameters were used. Where there is a reasonable basis to consider searching backup or preservation repositories, decisions to search or not to search those locations should be documented by the responsible officials. The ability to demonstrate a reasonable search process is particularly important when records have been disposed of in accordance with the institution’s approved retention schedules and disposition authorizations before a request is anticipated or received. It is a best practice for institutions to establish governance structures and guidance to support consistent search practices while recognizing that the scope and methodology of a search will be determined based on the circumstances of each request.

In the event of a missing records complaint under the Act, the Office of the Information Commissioner has authority under section 36 of the Act to examine any record under the control of a government institution and may compel the institution to conduct searches beyond those undertaken during original processing, including in backup storage locations. Institutions are encouraged to ensure that their retention and archiving approach for M365 content is sufficient to support a meaningful search at the time such a compelled examination might occur.

### 5.5 Managing records once an access to information request is received or a legal hold is in place

Once an ATI request is received, OPIs are generally responsible for identifying responsive records in their area of responsibility while ATIP officials, IM specialists, legal services and information technology personnel provide support consistent with their mandates. Institutions must take appropriate measures to preserve any records that may fall within the scope of that request. It is a best practice for ATIP coordinators and offices to communicate clearly and promptly to relevant OPIs when a request is received so that employees can assess the scope of responsive records and ensure they are preserved during the processing period. When records are held for the purpose of an ATI request, they must not be disposed of, including records in Microsoft Teams and other M365 services. Destroying responsive records with the intent of denying access is prohibited by the Act.

Timely implementation of legal holds is equally important and can be achieved through departmental litigation readiness efforts. It is recommended that institutions establish a departmental litigation readiness governance structure to identify clear roles and responsibilities in the institution with respect to implementing and managing legal holds once an implementation notice is received from the Department of Justice Canada. The governance structure can also be responsible for notifying employees of active legal holds that could apply to records they create or that are under their control. Under the LAC [Directive on Litigation Support](https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/corporate/about-us/policies/litigation-support.html), government institutions must also inform LAC if a preservation order or a litigation hold letter (or notice) affects dormant records stored at LAC on their behalf.

Implementation delays can result in the loss of potentially responsive records before a search can be conducted and could have significant legal repercussions. Conversely, establishing processes to lift holds promptly once formal notice is received from the Department of Justice Canada may help to avoid indefinitely retaining records that no longer require preservation.

Pursuant to section 4.4.8 of the [Directive on Service and Digital](https://www.tbs-sct.canada.ca/pol/doc-eng.aspx?id=32601), government institutions must have documented processes in place to ensure that legal holds and disposition suspensions are applied in a targeted and timely manner when there is a reasonable expectation that records may be subject to an ATI request or legal proceeding. Institutions may identify and preserve specific responsive record sets defined by custodian, date range, content type and subject matter. Preservation measures should be implemented in a timely and targeted manner: M365 eDiscovery tools support this kind of targeted preservation using hold policies scoped to users, date ranges and keywords, which institutions may leverage in preference to broader suspension mechanisms when technically and practically feasible. Where technical preservation tools are used, they may be most effective in supporting, rather than defining, the institution’s preservation and records management obligations.

### 5.6 Training and awareness

Sound IM in digital collaboration environments depends on employees having practical knowledge of their obligations and of the tools available to meet them. Institutions are encouraged to incorporate guidance on the management of transitory records in collaboration tools into their existing ATI, privacy and IM training programs.

Pursuant to section 4.1.2 and Appendix B of the directive, all employees of government institutions must receive training on their obligations under the Act, the application of the Act, and Treasury Board policies. This training must cover specific institutional policies and processes relating to the administration of the Act, including policies on IM. Institutions may also wish to ensure that the training addresses:

- the definition of transitory records and how to apply it in the context of Microsoft Teams and other M365 tools
- procedures and best practices for migrating records of business value created in collaboration tools to designated corporate repositories
- the expectation that employees will proactively dispose of transitory records, except under specific circumstances, and how to do so appropriately

## 6. Application

This implementation notice applies to government institutions as defined in section 3 of the Act, including parent Crown corporations and any wholly owned subsidiaries, but does not apply to the Bank of Canada.

The [Directive on Service and Digital](https://www.tbs-sct.canada.ca/pol/doc-eng.aspx?id=32601) applies directly only to departments as defined in section 2 and entities listed in Schedules I, I.1 and II of the Financial Administration Act, as well as any other entity named in Schedule IV of that Act. Where institutions subject to the Act are not governed by the Directive on Service and Digital, they are to refer to their institution’s relevant internal policies and directives on information management.

All institutions are encouraged to manage their records according to the methodologies, mechanisms and tools outlined in section 4.4 of the [Directive on Service and Digital](https://www.tbs-sct.canada.ca/pol/doc-eng.aspx?id=32601) (Open and strategic management of information and data).
