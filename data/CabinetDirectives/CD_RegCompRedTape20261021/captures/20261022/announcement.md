# Government of Canada launches new directive to reduce red tape and strengthen competitiveness

From: [Treasury Board of Canada Secretariat](https://www.canada.ca/en/treasury-board-secretariat.html)

## News release

Government of Canada launches new directive to reduce red tape and strengthen competitiveness

October 21, 2026 – Ottawa, Ontario – Treasury Board of Canada Secretariat

Following the introduction of the Cutting Red Tape for all Canadians Act, the Government of Canada is taking further action to eliminate unnecessary regulatory burden and strengthen competitiveness.

Today, the Honourable Shafqat Ali, President of the Treasury Board, announced a new Cabinet Directive on Regulatory Competitiveness and Red Tape Reduction.

The Directive changes the foundation of how the government creates regulations. It will require regulators to reduce regulatory burden costs by $2 for every $1 added. In addition, it requires regulators to:

- assess the impacts of regulatory proposals on competitiveness
- remove outdated rules as quickly as possible
- use recognized national or international standards by default
- regularly review regulations to ensure they remain effective and up to date

By improving the predictability and efficiency of federal regulations, the government is making it easier for businesses to invest, innovate and grow, while maintaining effective protections for the health and safety of Canadians and the environment.

## Quick facts

- The New Cabinet directive takes effect on December 31, 2026, replacing the 2018 Cabinet Directive on Regulation.
- Supplementary resources that will outline requirements for developing regulatory proposals will be made available shortly. Key obligations from the current directive related to good regulatory practices will continue to apply.
- Under the new Cabinet directive, regulators must uphold all obligations to First Nations, Inuit, and Métis communities.
- The directive also requires regulatory proposals and decisions to be based on an assessment of cost, benefit and risk, as well as impacts on competitiveness and diverse groups of Canadians.
- On December 3, 2025, the President of the Treasury Board hosted a Red Tape Reduction Summit in Ottawa where he launched horizontal red tape reviews, building on the 60-day Red Tape Review in Summer 2025.
- On October 7, 2026, the Government of Canada introduced Bill S-7, the Cutting Red Tape for All Canadians Act, which targets amendments to 31 pieces of legislation to remove regulatory burden and red tape.
- Since the publication of the September 8, 2025, Red Tape Progress Reports, the government has continued to advance the nearly 500 identified achievements and initiatives. To date, more than 160 of these have been completed and implementation remains underway across departments and agencies.
